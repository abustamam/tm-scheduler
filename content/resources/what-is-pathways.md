# What is Pathways?

**Pathways** is Toastmasters International's learning experience — the modern
curriculum members use to build speaking and leadership skills at their own pace.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

- You choose a **path** aligned with your goals. There are several to pick from, each focused on a theme such as presentation skills, leadership, or persuasion.
- Each path is organised into **five levels**, moving from foundational skills to more advanced projects.
- Each level contains **projects** — a mix of required and elective — and most projects are completed by giving a speech at a club meeting.
- You track your progress online through **Base Camp**, Toastmasters' learning portal.

## Your first few weeks {#first-weeks}

Nobody expects you to have it all figured out on day one. Most new members
settle in over their first month or so, roughly in this order:

1. **Choose a path.** Pick the one that sounds closest to what you want to get
   better at. You're not locked in forever — see [Choosing a path](#choosing-a-path)
   below.
2. **Give your Ice Breaker.** Every path opens with the same first project: a
   short speech introducing yourself to the club. It's the friendliest audience
   you'll ever have, and nobody is grading you on polish. Let whoever plans your
   club's agenda know when you'd like a slot.
3. **Take a supporting role.** Before or after your Ice Breaker, sign up for one
   of the smaller meeting roles, such as timer or grammarian. It's a low-pressure
   way to get on your feet, learn how a meeting flows, and meet people. The
   [meeting roles guide](/resources/meeting-roles) explains each one.
4. **Set up Base Camp.** Sign in, open your path, and find your first project.
   The steps are [further down this page](#base-camp).

## Choosing a path {#choosing-a-path}

When you join, a short assessment recommends paths based on what you want to work
on. There's no wrong choice — every path builds core communication and
leadership skills, and you can take more than one over time.

## Setting up Base Camp {#base-camp}

Base Camp is where your path actually lives: the project descriptions, the
objectives for each speech, and the record of which levels you've finished.
Getting in usually takes a few minutes:

1. **Wait for your membership to go through.** Your club sends your membership
   to Toastmasters International, and your member account is created once it's
   processed. If you haven't had a welcome email after a week or two, ask one of
   your club's officers.
2. **Sign in with your member account.** Go to
   [basecamp.toastmasters.org](https://basecamp.toastmasters.org) and sign in with
   your toastmasters.org account, using the email you joined with. If you've
   never signed in before, you may need to create or reset a password for that
   account first.
3. **Check your path.** If you chose a path when you joined, it should already
   be there. If you haven't chosen one yet, choose it from your Toastmasters
   account on toastmasters.org. Your club's Vice President Education can help if
   you get stuck.
4. **Open Level 1 and find your first project.** Read through what the Ice
   Breaker asks of you before you write your speech — each project has its own
   objectives, and your evaluator will be listening for them.
5. **Mark projects complete as you go.** After you give a speech, record it in
   Base Camp. When you've finished a level, your club's Vice President Education
   usually confirms it there.

If something looks wrong in Base Camp, such as the wrong path or a missing
level, your Vice President Education is the person to ask.

## How your club tracks your progress {#tracking-progress}

There are two places your progress shows up, and they do different jobs.

**Base Camp is the official record.** The project material, your completed
projects and your level completions all live there, and that's what
Toastmasters International counts.

**GavelUp is your club's day-to-day view.** If your club uses GavelUp, it shows
which path you're on, how far through each level you are, and the speeches you
have coming up on future agendas. It's a companion to Base Camp, not a copy of
it: it doesn't hold any of the project material, and nothing you do in GavelUp
updates Base Camp, so you still record your projects there. You can tick off
projects in GavelUp yourself, and your club's officers help keep it current. If
the two ever disagree, Base Camp is the one to trust, and it's worth letting an
officer know.

## Why members like it {#why-members-like-it}

Pathways turns "I want to get better at speaking" into a concrete sequence of
projects, each with clear objectives and built-in feedback from your club. You
always know what you're working on next.
